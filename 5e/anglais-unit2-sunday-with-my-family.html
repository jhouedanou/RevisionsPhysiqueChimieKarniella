Session 1 — B/ Language function

★ Describing everyday activities with the simple present

Pour dire ce que tu fais d'habitude (chaque jour, chaque dimanche), utilise le présent simple.

1. Affirmative form

📘 La structure

Subject + verb

Avec he, she, it, le verbe prend un -s.

SubjectExample
I / You / We / They + verbI wake up at six every Sunday.
He / She / It + verb-SMy mother cooks rice every Sunday.

✍️ Comment écrire le -s

  • En général, + s : play → plays, cook → cooks.
  • Verbe en -o, -s, -sh, -ch, -x : + es : go → goes, wash → washes, watch → watches.
  • Consonne + y : y → ies : study → studies.
  • have → has.

2. Negative form

📘 La structure

Subject + don't / doesn't + verb

don't = do not (I, you, we, they). doesn't = does not (he, she, it).

  • I don't sweep the yard on Sundays.
  • My brother doesn't wash the dishes.

3. Interrogative form

📘 La structure

Do / Does + subject + verb ?

Réponses courtes : Yes, I do. / No, I don't. Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.

A: Do you go to church on Sundays?

B: Yes, I do.

A: What does your father do on Sundays?

B: He visits our relatives.

4. Les mots de l'habitude

WordEn français
every daychaque jour
every Sunday / on Sundayschaque dimanche, le dimanche
in the morningle matin
in the afternoonl'après-midi
in the eveningle soir

⚠️ Les deux fautes

  • Oublier le -s : She cook → She cooks.
  • Garder le -s après doesn't : She doesn't cooks → She doesn't cook.

🐴 Le S voyage

Avec he, she, it, il faut un S. Soit sur le verbe (she cooks), soit sur does (she doesn't cook). Jamais les deux.

Session 2 — B/ Language function

★ Describing past actions with the simple past (preterit)

Pour raconter ce que tu as fait (hier, dimanche dernier), utilise le prétérit. Le verbe a la même forme pour toutes les personnes.

1. Affirmative form

a) Regular verbs : verb + ED

VerbExample
visit → visitedWe visited our grandparents last Sunday.
play → playedI played with my cousins yesterday.
dance → dancedMy sister danced with her friends.
study → studiedAya studied her lessons last night.

✍️ Comment écrire le -ed

  • En général, + ed : visit → visited.
  • Verbe en -e : + d : dance → danced.
  • Consonne + y : y → ied : study → studied.

b) Irregular verbs : à apprendre par cœur

VerbSimple pastEn français
gowentaller
havehadavoir
eatatemanger
seesawvoir

dodidfaire
get upgot upse lever
taketookprendre
telltoldraconter, dire
buyboughtacheter
  • We went to the beach last Sunday.
  • I had lunch with my family.

2. Negative form

📘 La structure

Subject + didn't (did not) + verb

  • I didn't go to school yesterday.
  • She didn't eat fish.

3. Interrogative form

📘 La structure

Did + subject + verb ?

Réponses courtes : Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.

A: Did you visit your grandparents last Sunday?

B: Yes, I did.

A: What did you eat?

B: We ate rice and chicken.

⚠️ Après did et didn't

Le verbe reste à la base : She didn't went → She didn't go. Did you saw → Did you see.

🐴 Did porte le passé

Did est le passé de do. Il porte déjà le passé, alors le verbe derrière reste nu.

C/ Practice

Cherche d'abord seule, à voix haute si tu peux. Ensuite, ouvre la correction.

Exercise 1 — Simple present

Mets le verbe entre parenthèses au présent simple.

  1. Aya … (wake up) at six every Sunday.
  2. We … (go) to church in the morning.
  3. My father … (wash) the car on Sundays.
  4. My little sister … (study) in the evening.
  5. They … (not / sweep) the yard.
Voir la correction
  1. wakes up
  2. go
  3. washes
  4. studies
  5. don't sweep

Exercise 2 — Simple past

Mets le verbe entre parenthèses au prétérit.

  1. Yesterday, I … (visit) my aunt.
  2. Last Sunday, we … (go) to the beach.
  3. She … (eat) fish yesterday.
  4. They … (play) games last week.
  5. My father … (take) photos.
Voir la correction
  1. visited
  2. went
  3. ate
  4. played
  5. took

Exercise 3 — Negative and question

Écris chaque phrase à la forme négative, puis à la forme interrogative.

  1. She went to church.
  2. They had lunch together.
  3. You saw your cousins.
Voir la correction
  1. She didn't go to church. — Did she go to church?
  2. They didn't have lunch together. — Did they have lunch together?
  3. You didn't see your cousins. — Did you see your cousins?

Exercise 4 — Session 3: Aya's Sunday

Lis le dialogue, puis réponds aux questions en anglais.

Karniella: What did you do last Sunday, Aya?

Aya: I visited my grandparents in the village.

Karniella: Did your parents go with you?

Aya: Yes, they did. We had lunch together, and my grandfather told stories.

Karniella: And what do you usually do on Sundays?

Aya: I go to church in the morning, and I watch TV in the evening.

  1. Who did Aya visit last Sunday?
  2. Who told stories?
  3. What does Aya do in the evening on Sundays?
Voir la correction
  1. She visited her grandparents.
  2. Her grandfather told stories.
  3. She watches TV.

Question 3 : usually (d'habitude) → présent simple, avec un -s : she watches.

À toi : réponds pour toi
  • What do you do on Sundays? → I …
  • What did you do last Sunday? → I …
